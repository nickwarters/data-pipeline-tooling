Halving the framework
where 4,656 lines can go

A line-by-line reduction plan for framework/, tools/ and cli/ on the assumption that the eager authoring model stays and the deferred graph does not. What is genuinely load-bearing, what is capability nobody has ever called, and what is simply verbose.

2026-08-25 9,517 SLOC · 63 modules · 126 exported names branch t3code/simplify-eager-pipeline-framework AST-measured, not estimated

The verdict in four sentences

50% is reachable, and roughly a third of it is free. Of the 126 names the four facades export, 49 are reachable from production pipelines; 12 more are reached only by demo pipelines, 56 only by tests, and 9 by nothing at all. 1,577 SLOC is dead capability — real, documented, tested API with no caller anywhere outside tests/ — and deleting it loses nothing that is in use today.

The next 1,928 SLOC is genuine capability that has to be retired by decision: the graph builder, the deadline-ordering half of the orchestrator, the row trace, and the SharePoint client that belongs in the one pipeline that uses it. The last 1,151 SLOC is pure density — 1,311 of the 9,517 lines are a lone closing bracket or a single argument being passed through, and the run-record stack spends 684 lines on a 20-field append-only log. Together: 4,656 SLOC, 48.9%. Anything beyond that starts cutting the schema contract, which is the part of this framework actually worth having.

The three dispositions

Every line on the cut list falls into one of three buckets, and the distinction is the whole point of this report. They carry very different risk:

Tier A — dead

No reference from pipelines/, case_review/, readers/, shared/, cli/, or from any other module in framework/ or tools/. Only its own definition, its facade export, and tests. Deleting it costs nothing in use today. 1,577 SLOC.

Tier B — relaxation

Real capability with real callers. Removing it retires a decision and, in four cases, an ADR. Each item here is a question for you, not an action for me. 1,928 SLOC.

Tier C — density

Same behaviour, fewer lines. Signature flattening, one run-record store instead of two, one scaffold template instead of two. No capability changes hands. 1,151 SLOC.

Keep

The kernel — the schema contract, the eager steps, the three readers and four writers in use, migrations, the run log. 4,861 SLOC.

§ 3

Four findings that carry the cut

1. Streaming has no callers — and there are two of it

Pipeline.read_chunks is described in CLAUDE.md as a first-class primitive and costs the builder its four wiring-time refusals. No pipeline calls it. Neither does anything call stream_step in tools/observability/stream.py — a second, independent streaming drive written because the graph builder is single-shot and a ChunkReader could not be wired into it. Two implementations of one unused feature.

The cost is not mainly lines. It is that streaming is the sole reason UniqueValidator vs StreamingUniqueValidator is a choice an author must make, the sole reason a Writer needs a writing_chunks() session, and the sole reason four pairings are refused at wiring time. Under the eager model, streaming is a for loop over reader.chunks(n) — about 40 lines when a feed is genuinely too big, not a subsystem carried in advance.

2. Production speaks 41 words; the demos speak the rest

The three pipelines named in case_review/schedules.py — sharepoint_cases, reviewer_activity, complaint_selection — plus case_review/ and readers/, between them import 41 distinct names from framework.* and tools.*. Everything else is exercised by demos, and several demos exist specifically to exercise it. pipelines/ordering_demo and the seven demo_* feeds exist to demonstrate the orchestrator's deadline-priority ordering; that ordering has no production declaration. That is circular justification, and it is worth naming.

3. The most-churned file is the largest deletion

4. A tenth of the code is line-wrapping

Of 9,517 executable lines, 515 are nothing but a closing bracket and 796 are a single identifier or key=value being passed through — 13.8% between them. This is not a formatting complaint; it is a signature complaint. The clearest specimen is Orchestrator._decide_item: 117 SLOC, of which nine call sites repeat the same eight positional arguments to _decision(...). Binding them once collapses it to roughly 45 with no behaviour change at all.

§ 8

The order to do it in

Sequencing matters because several cuts unlock others, and because the early steps are the ones that can be verified by the suite still passing.

  1. Tier A, in one pass — 1,577 SLOC. Delete the unused readers, writers, strategies, processors, validators, profile.py, retry.py, discovery.py, remote.py, stream.py and the six registry queries, with their tests and doc sections. Nothing else depends on the order; every deletion is independently verifiable by the suite. This is the whole of the safe third and it should be a separate PR.
  2. Relocate the SharePoint client — 355 SLOC. Pure move into pipelines/sharepoint_cases/. Do it before the builder work so the largest remaining tools/ module is out of the way.
  3. Convert the two remaining production graph pipelines. reviewer_activity/gold.py and report_feed.py to eager steps. Then the demos. Argument order is already identical.
  4. Delete the builder — 624 SLOC + 129 of describe(). Only after (3). This is the step that touches 41 test files; budget for it accordingly.
  5. Orchestration — 464 SLOC. Independent of everything above. Start with the mechanical _decide_item collapse (117 → ~45, no behaviour change) to make the rest readable, then remove ordering, overrides and the unused schedules.
  6. CLI and scaffold — 518 SLOC. Last, because the scaffold templates should be written against the framework you end up with, not the one you started from.
  7. The density pass — 1,151 SLOC. Genuinely last, and honestly the most likely to be descoped. The run-record consolidation is the one piece here with real value beyond line count.

Steps 1 and 2 alone are 1,932 lines — 20% — and neither needs a decision from anyone.

§ 9

What I would not cut, and why

The declared-schema contract

412 SLOC across _internal/schema, core/schema, value_rules and coercion. This is the framework's actual value: coerce → quarantine → validate, declared once on a dataclass. Everything else here is plumbing around it.

The migration guard

_MigrationGuard and the schema_migrations ledger check. It is what stops a nightly Refresh silently dropping a migration's primary key and indexes. Small, load-bearing, and its failure mode is invisible.

AppendOnly's conflict detection

90 of that writer's 117 SLOC are _collapsed, _refuse_narrowed_batch and _refuse_changed_rows. It looks like a lot for one strategy. It is the only thing standing between an overlapping poll and a silently corrupted immutable table — worth confirming you want, not worth cutting unasked.

--dry-run

73 SLOC. Tempting because DryRunReport is on the tests-only list, but it is reachable from the operator CLI, which is how it is meant to be used. Cutting it because no Python calls it would be reading the census wrong.

The Dataset seam

39 SLOC to keep pandas out of every signature in the tree (ADR-0002). The cheapest architectural boundary here by a wide margin.

One run identity

Whatever happens to the run-record stack, the fix recorded in the simplification review — every main() routing through the same run_pipeline — must survive it. Two run identities for one feed broke freshness and turned re-drives into duplicate accumulation, and nothing looked wrong.

§ 10

Caveats, and where this could be wrong

  • Tier C is an estimate, not an inventory. Tiers A and B are itemised down to named symbols and I would defend those numbers. Tier C's 400-line flattening figure is 30% of a measured 1,311 — a judgement about recoverability, not a count. If it comes in at half, the plan lands at 45% rather than 49%.
  • "No caller" means no caller today. The census is static and covers this repository. A SAS feed, an Excel source or a monthly upsert that is planned but not yet written would make several Tier A items wrong. The SAS entry (ADR-0012) is the one I would check with a person before deleting.
  • The 41-file test conversion is not in the 4,656. It is the largest piece of work in the plan and it produces no line reduction in the measured scope.
  • Relocating the SharePoint client reduces the framework, not the repository. 355 of the 4,656 move rather than disappear. If your target is repository-wide rather than framework-wide, subtract them and the figure is 45.2%.
  • This corroborates rather than discovers. docs/framework-simplification-review.md reached the same conclusions about the graph and streaming by different measurements, and left a nine-item backlog of which none is implemented. What is new here is the line budget, the three-tier confidence split, and the finding that stream.py is a second unused streaming implementation.
  • The test the review recommended still stands, and beats this document. Have the engineer who will maintain the monthly three-file feed write it against this branch. What they reach for and cannot find is a better cut list than anything derived by parsing.
